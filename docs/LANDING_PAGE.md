# Side — Landing Page (Waitlist v1)

Scope: single page, one goal — collect waitlist emails. No pricing, no docs, no feature tour. Ship something honest about where the product actually is (early, opinionated, in active development) rather than overselling a finished IDE.

---

## Meta

- **Title:** Side — a focused, agent-assisted dev environment for Mac
- **Description:** Side is a native Mac IDE built around tracks, not panels. Join the waitlist.
- **Favicon / OG image:** wordmark on dark background, keep it minimal — no screenshot yet unless it's genuinely presentable.

**Microcopy under the form:**

Keep the hero to headline + subhead + form. Nothing else above the fold — this is the one screen most visitors will actually read.

---

## Section: What's different (3 short columns, not paragraphs)

**One stage owns the screen.**
No permanent file tree, no chat rail bolted to the side. Side shows you what matters for what you're doing right now.

**Agents propose, you decide.**
Agent-made changes arrive as inspectable checkpoints — a diff, a rationale, evidence. Nothing lands without you promoting it.

**Git is the substrate, not a plugin.**
Tracks are reviewable paths back to `main`. Side interoperates with ordinary commits, branches, and pull requests — it doesn't reinvent them.

*(Three columns, icon or single-color glyph above each, one sentence + one supporting line. Resist the urge to add a fourth — three reads as intentional, four starts to feel like a feature list.)*

---

## Section: Why now (short, one paragraph, optional — cut if the page feels long)

> Most IDEs were designed before agents were part of the workflow, so agent chat got bolted on as another panel. Side starts from the other direction: what does a Mac development environment look like if agent-assisted work was the default, not an add-on?

## Tone notes for whoever builds this

- Visually: dark, quiet, monospace accents somewhere (this is a dev tool) — but don't over-design a one-screen waitlist page. It should look like it took a day, not a month.
- Don't show a product screenshot unless the Make stage genuinely looks polished enough to represent the product well — a bad screenshot undersells more than no screenshot.
- Copy voice: direct and a little opinionated (matches the constitution's own tone), not marketing-speak. "A Mac IDE built around your work, not your windows" over "The next-generation AI-powered development platform."
